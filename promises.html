What trustees promised, and how they voted

Updated November 27, 2026

Each trustee's campaign commitments, in their own words from public voter guides, next to their recorded votes from October 20, 2025 through October 27, 2026. Quotes are short excerpts; follow the link to read each full answer. Vote counts come from the Board's signed minutes, listed vote by vote on the votes page.

The short version

Several trustees ran on transparency, cost cutting or lower taxes. In 13 months and 121 recorded votes, 112 were unanimous, and no trustee voted against a budget, a tax rate, a contract or a purchase. On August 17, 2026 every trustee voted to keep the tax rate unchanged, which raised the school tax bill on an average home by $141 because values rose.

Stephanie Elad

Place 3, elected 2022, reelected 2025

What they told voters

  • Supports lowering the property tax rate.
  • Was the only trustee to vote against putting the 2024 bond on the ballot, citing the price, added debt, and items she called wants rather than needs.
  • Said residents feel the 2018 bond's costs and spending were never properly accounted for.

Source: Dallas Morning News voter guide, 2025

What the record shows

100 votes cast: 96 yes, 3 no, 1 abstain. Her no votes were on two library materials lists and a missed school day waiver; the abstention was on a library list. She voted yes on the August 17, 2026 resolution that kept the tax rate unchanged. She is not listed on the June 15, 2026 budget adoption vote.

Renee Sample

Place 2, elected 2025

What they told voters

  • Listed fiscal transparency as a priority, with “an efficient, sustainable budget that prioritizes student focused funding.”
  • Said that if state funding stayed flat, “a modest increase in the tax rate may be necessary.”
  • Served on the last bond committee and said the program needed better timing and communication, with community input gathered before committee work starts.

Source: Dallas Morning News voter guide, 2025

What the record shows

121 votes cast: 118 yes, 2 no, 1 abstain. Her no votes were on a student expulsion appeal and a student grievance appeal. She abstained on November 17, 2025 budget transfers and amendments, the only vote on a budget item that was not unanimous. She voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget.

Suresh Manduva

Place 1, elected 2025

What they told voters

  • If the Legislature did not raise funding enough, he would start with “a full audit of current spending,” protecting core academics and teacher pay while postponing nonessential services.
  • Would support a lower tax rate only if classroom funding and teacher pay are not harmed.
  • Described the District's bond management as generally strong.

Source: Dallas Morning News voter guide, 2025

What the record shows

117 votes cast: 115 yes, 2 no. Both no votes were on student grievance appeals. The minutes show the routine annual financial audit and internal audit plan, both approved unanimously, but no vote on a separate audit of current spending. He voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget.

Keith Maddox

Place 7, elected 2024

What they told voters

  • Named state funding as the top issue: “It's time for the legislature to do theirs.”
  • Said all cost saving measures are “on the table.”
  • Said launching new programs while facing deficits “is not prudent.”

Source: Dallas Morning News voter guide, 2024

What the record shows

119 votes cast: 119 yes, 0 no. He voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget.

Sherrie Salas

Place 6, elected 2024

What they told voters

  • Find efficiencies and cut costs without larger class sizes or layoffs, guided by a committee of staff and stakeholders.
  • Said new spending requires cuts elsewhere or tax increases, which “no one wants.”
  • “Be transparent and inform staff, parents, and stakeholders of changes being considered.”

Source: Dallas Morning News voter guide, 2024

What the record shows

116 votes cast: 116 yes, 0 no. She voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget.

Dynette Davis

Place 4, elected 2020, reelected 2026

What they told voters

  • Says she kept the District's tax rate “one of the lowest in the area” and balanced the budget in a fiscally responsible way.
  • Says her budget work increased community transparency and trust.
  • As Board president, led the search for the new superintendent.

Source: Branch candidate profile, 2026

What the record shows

114 votes cast: 114 yes, 0 no. She voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget.

Misty Wamhoff

Place 5, elected 2026

What they told voters

  • Public budget and enrollment dashboards.
  • A formal process for handling parent concerns.
  • Candid reporting on District performance.
  • Stated no position on tax rates or bonds.

Source: Branch candidate profile, 2026

What the record shows

52 votes cast since she joined the Board in May 2026: 51 yes, 1 no. Her no vote was on the October 2026 library materials list. She voted yes on the 2026 tax rate and the 2026-27 budget. Whether the District has published a public budget dashboard since she took office is not verified.

Notes

  • Mark Hill left the Board in May 2026 and is not included.
  • Campaign statements are quoted or summarized from the linked voter guides. Each trustee is welcome to send a fuller account of their commitments and where each one stands, and we will post it here unedited.
  • A yes vote on a budget or tax rate is not by itself a broken promise. The point is to compare what was said with what the record shows, and let voters ask the questions.
  • Earlier signed minutes are not posted on the District's meeting site, so votes before October 20, 2025 are not included.
